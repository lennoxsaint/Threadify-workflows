# 6. Your voice

## Principle

Your voice is how you sound when you are not trying to sound like a writer. The test is simple: would you say this out loud to a friend who does what you do?

Basics that help almost everyone:

- Short lines.
- Plain words.
- First person, from what you have actually done or seen.
- A small admission before the lesson.
- A last line that ends the post cleanly and does not wrap it in a moral.

Then choose your own habits and keep them. Lennox writes in lowercase and uses " - " where other people use a long dash. Those are his habits. You do not have to copy them. Pick yours and be consistent, so people can recognise a post as yours before they see the name.

Go easy on words that show up in every generic post: unlock, leverage, game-changer, journey, landscape.

## Worked example

An example written for this lesson, not a real post.

Stiff:

> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, consistency is the key to unlocking growth.

Said out loud:

> i missed four days last month and nothing bad happened.
> i just felt weird about it.

## Common mistake

Borrowing a voice. Copying the rhythm of a bigger account until your posts could be anyone's.

## On the learner's posts

Connected: read their best posts with `greatest_hits`. Ask the learner to pick the one that sounds most like them talking and say what it does that the others do not. Write those habits into `NOTES.md`.

Not connected: same with the pasted sample. If they have no posts, ask them to explain their work in a voice note or a few typed lines, as if to a friend, and work from that.

## Practice task

Take your stiffest post. Read it out loud. Rewrite every line you would not actually say.

## Lesson post

Write a post the way you would say it. Read it out loud before you finalise it.

## Edge of the free material

This lesson covers the basics of finding and keeping a voice. Having a tool learn your voice from your whole history is outside it. Use the one-line pointer if the learner asks.
