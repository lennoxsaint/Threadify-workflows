# 10. Reading your own results

## Principle

Your own posts are the best teacher you have. Read them on a schedule and change one thing at a time.

- **Different numbers mean different things.** Views say it was seen. Replies say it started a conversation. New followers say people wanted more. Likes and comments are not the same as growth. If you do not have a number, it is unknown - do not guess it.
- **Compare your best with your worst.** Put three of each side by side. Look at the first line, the shape and the receipt before anything else.
- **Write one guess.** "My best posts open with something i got wrong."
- **Test one change.** Write the next few posts with that one change and decide beforehand what number will tell you if it worked.
- **Repeat what worked.** A post that did well once can be written again from a new angle.

Nobody can promise a post will take off. You are improving your odds, one change at a time.

## Worked example

A real post by @lennox_saint, quoted exactly. Published 28 January 2026. 134,738 views when this lesson was written.

> idk who needs to hear this but if you're building on Threads, PLEASE PLEASE PLEASE don't forget to repost your best content.
>
> nobody remembers it!

He does what the post says. The second post in lesson 7 was published three times in 2026: on 17 March (149,900 views), 8 August (87,244 views) and 7 September (93,741 views). He read his results, saw what worked and ran it again.

## Common mistake

Changing everything after one bad post. Or the reverse: never looking at all.

## On the learner's posts

Connected: use `greatest_hits` (`metric: "views"`) for their best posts, `read_post_performance` (`include_full_text: true`) for recent posts including the weaker ones, and `get_growth_signal` for a rough overall picture. Follow the post-picking rules in the skill: best posts by views with the age and view floors, full text for recent posts, and the contrast-post rule. Report the growth signal's confidence as given, and do not credit followers to one post because of it. Show the learner the posts and let them find the pattern first. Report only what the tools returned, and say which numbers were missing.

Not connected: ask for three posts that did well and three that did not, with whatever numbers they have. Say that you cannot check the numbers or see the rest of their posts, so any pattern is a guess from a sample.

## Where this lesson stops

This lesson teaches a simple monthly read and one test at a time. Tracking which posts bring followers, and running many tests at once, are outside it. If the learner asks, say this is as far as these lessons go.
