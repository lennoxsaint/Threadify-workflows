# Threads generation playbook

These are generation instructions. Do not run editorial audits or automatic rewrite loops after generation. Use the owner's voice, casing choice and approved destination.

## Hook
Short-form: exactly one curiosity-opening sentence, then directly into the body. Allude to something the body answers. One-liners may stand alone without a forced explanation. Use a number only if it really happened.

## Hold
Pay off the opening curiosity with concrete, grounded information. Remove filler hedging without hiding uncertainty. Use specific stakes and a clear sequence rather than repeated proof.

## Help
Give a specific usable payoff. Hook/hold/help and proof/promise/plan apply where grounded; never invent proof to complete a formula.

## CTA
Place a relevant CTA after value, using the owner's approved destination. The RSS test: is it relevant, specific and a sensible next step? Respect an explicit workflow-specific clean link over broader attribution instructions. Do not add tracking parameters without approval or change account settings.

## Structures
Read an actual successful source post in full, extract its mechanics, and adapt those mechanics to the creator's facts. The Rule of Two for long-form means two open loops, both resolved by the body. Assign a distinct angle, hook mechanism and evidence before generating each slot. Generic examples are not proof of performance.

## Short-form types
Listicles go directly from the one-sentence hook into the numbered list. No analogy, setup paragraph, second intro or “here are the five” transition. Other short posts go straight into the body. A standalone one-liner needs no appended explanation.

## Long-form
The opening post is exactly three sentences. Sentence one is short and opens a story or curiosity loop. Sentence two supplies stakes or context without resolving it. Sentence three opens the deliverable loop, promises a specific reason to reveal the next post, and ends with a colon. The body resolves both loops.

Choose a genuinely new subject or refresh an existing published thread with verified current facts and preserved provenance. Never invent updated metrics. When revising an existing thread, aim for approximately 30% shorter body parts against the recorded original. For a new thread, aim for 22–48 words per body part; do not claim an unmeasured reduction.

## Generation input
Include: owner voice; assigned topic and unique angle; actual source template and relevant adaptation; assigned proof and its source; planned content type; the hook format above; specific payoff; approved CTA destination after value; strict facts; and unique attempt marker. Read current brand doctrine when available. Retain the original response unchanged except for chosen casing or an explicit owner edit.
