# 9. Commenting on other people's posts

## Principle

Commenting is how new people meet you. The right comment depends on the room.

- **Big rooms** - large accounts and posts that are already taking off. Hundreds of comments. What stands out is one sharp line, a careful correction or a useful addition.
- **Small rooms** - smaller accounts and quieter posts. What matters is the relationship: something specific, some support, a bit of your own story or a real question.

Do not use the same style in both.

Four comments worth learning:

- **Useful add-on** - a missing step, example or warning. It should make the original author look good.
- **Kind correction** - "yes, and the part people miss is...". Short. No dunking.
- **Your own story** - one line of what happened to you, one line of what you took from it.
- **One sharp line** - for crowded posts. No explanation.

Only comment when you have something to add. A comment made just to be seen reads that way.

## Worked example

A real post by @lennox_saint, quoted exactly. Published 5 June 2026. 7,311 views and 84 replies when this lesson was written. It states the idea behind this lesson.

> don't build an audience on Threads.
>
> build community.

Commenting is where that happens.

Other people's posts are not quoted in these lessons, so the exchange below is made up to show the difference.

Post, from a small account: "three months of posting and i still feel like i'm talking to myself."

Weak comment:

> keep going, you've got this!

Better comment, for a small room:

> month three was the worst one for me too. the thing that helped was replying to five people a day before i posted anything. who do you wish was reading?

## Common mistake

Hijacking. Turning someone else's post into an advert for yourself.

## On the learner's posts

This skill does not read other people's posts or send comments. Ask the learner to paste one post from a bigger account and one from a smaller account in their field. Decide together which room each one is and which kind of comment fits.

## Practice task

Write one comment for the big-room post and one for the small-room post. Check each one: could it sit under any post? If yes, rewrite it.

## Lesson post

Take the best idea from the comments you just wrote and turn it into a post of your own.

## Edge of the free material

This lesson covers the two rooms and four kinds of comment. Choosing who to comment on and measuring what it brings back are outside it. Use the one-line pointer if the learner asks.
