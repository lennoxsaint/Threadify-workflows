# Threadify Viral Posts

Start locally from confirmed facts and supplied posts without a Threadify account. Honor an existing connection choice. Offer a connection only when an available hosted capability would help; explain the benefit and obtain approval for the exact provider action.

Follow [Threadify-001: setup and first-loop video](references/threadify-001.md) before provider calls. Honor an explicit choice already given; setup never grants scheduling or publishing authority.

Use this when the creator says "viral posts", "make me viral posts" or "write posts from my viral templates". It makes posts only, for Threads only. It uses only the Threadify tools named below: no browser, no other scheduler and no other network.

## The five rules creators gave us

These come from real creators turning down drafts. Follow every one for every post.

1. **Copy the proven skeleton line for line.** A draft that is "not an exact template of posts that went viral" gets rejected. Keep the template's line count, line breaks, list shape, length (each line within its step-4 limit) and ending. Change only the filled-in words.
2. **Short hooks.** The hook (first line) is 80 characters or less, unless the template's own proven hook (the first line of its most-viewed evidence post) is longer. Then match its length, never go past it.
3. **The creator's energy, not a robot's.** Write in the voice and energy of the creator's own top posts. If a draft reads flat next to its evidence posts, it fails.
4. **Deliver what the hook promises.** Run a fresh-reader check: where your host can start a fresh agent or session, give it only this prompt, with the post's full text in place of `{POST}`:

   ```
   Use no tools. You are a stranger scrolling Threads and you see only this one post, nothing else about who wrote it:

   {POST}

   First decide what kind of post it is. If the opening line promises more to come (an explanation, a list, steps, a story, a reason, "here's why", "here's how", or it ends in a colon), the post delivers only if the rest gives exactly that. If the whole post is one complete observation or claim that promises nothing more, it delivers when you understand the whole claim without knowing the author; a bare claim with no reason given is fine.

   Answer in exactly four lines:
   PROMISE: <what the opening line promises the reader, in a few words>
   KIND: <exactly one of: promises more | complete claim>
   DELIVERS: <exactly one of: clear yes | borderline | no>
   WHY: <one sentence>

   Judge honestly. "clear yes" only if the post fully gives you what its opening promised without needing to know the author.
   ```

   Only `DELIVERS: clear yes` passes; `borderline` and `no` fail. The `KIND` line keeps one-line posts fair: a hook that promises more must get it in the rest of the post, while one complete claim passes when a stranger understands it, with no reason needed. Record who read it, its answer word for word, and its verdict: the word after `DELIVERS:`, copied from that answer, never your own summary of it. Your own read is never a pass: when no fresh agent or session is available, the check is **unavailable**. Read the post yourself with the same prompt, record your answer, and show "fresh reader: unavailable (self-read: <your answer>)" at the gate. Your own read can still fail a post: a self-read of `borderline` or `no` is a fail, so only a post you read as a clear yes is shown as "unavailable".
5. **Show every part of a thread.** At the approval gate, show the full text of every part, in order. Never show only the first post of a thread.

Never invent a number, quote, name or story. Every fact in a draft must come from the creator's Brain, their own posts or what they told you in this chat.

Copy shape only from posts you have read in full: every part of a thread, no cut-off text. Text that ends in "…" is cut off. Shape you have not seen is unverified, never an exact template.

## What the run saves before any schedule

Some calls save things in the creator's Threadify account before the approval gate. Say so before step 2 and ask for a yes to drafting on its own. That yes covers only these saves; it never covers scheduling.

- Browsing the viral library with `list_viral_items` stores a private audit record, and so does opening a post with `get_viral_item`, which also uses one viral allowance. This run opens at most 3 library posts: name that number in the drafting-yes ask, and opening more needs a new drafting yes that names them.
- Each `generate_content` call saves one draft and one chat in the account and uses one generation. The batch makes at most 2 versions per template, 6 calls in all. Name that number and the quota left.
- Versions that are not picked stay saved as drafts. List their draft ids at the gate and in the receipt, and leave them for the creator to keep or delete.

## Steps

1. **Connect check.** Call `get_connection_defaults` first. Read the account, timezone, scopes and generation quota from it. The **account** is its `account.username` with no @; use that same form in every fingerprint and key. Use that timezone for every time you show. Also read the global auto-repost setting (`automation.global_auto_repost`: on or off, and its trigger, delay or likes threshold). If it is missing or not `known`, it is unknown, never off. If there is no working connection, go to the local fallback below. Then ask for the drafting yes (above), naming `account.username` as the only account this run reads and writes. If the creator means another account, stop: it must become the connection default, and step 1 runs again. Without it, skip `list_viral_items`, `get_viral_item` and every later save, and stop after step 2 with evidence from the creator's own posts only.
2. **Find what went viral more than once.**
   - Call `greatest_hits` with `metric: "views"` and a `limit` of at least 20, all-time. Call `read_post_performance` with `all_time: true` and `sort_by: "views"`. Its `total_matched` is the account's post count; raise the `limit` until the post ranked at a tenth of that count, rounded down and at least 1 (rank 120 of 1,200; rank 1 for 1 to 19 posts), comes back. That post's views are the **top 10% line**. If `total_matched` is 0, there is nothing to copy: say so and stop before drafting. Record the count and the rank in the receipt. Use `search_posts` to find more posts that share a hook or shape you spotted.
   - For every evidence post, call `get_post_thread` to read every part in full and note its `media_type` and part count. The analytics tools return only the first part, and their text can be cut off. If an evidence post's later parts are only a plug (a link or a call to action) that the other evidence posts do not have, the template is the parts they share: say so at the gate and never copy the plug. If `get_post_thread` fails for an evidence post, its text is not read in full: label the template "shape unverified". If it returns no `media_type`, label the template "media unknown". Leave both out of this batch.
   - After the drafting yes only, call `list_viral_items` and `get_viral_item` to look at viral posts in the shared Viral library that match the creator's topics. Library posts only point you to shapes worth searching for in the creator's own posts. A shape seen only in the library is "unproven" for this creator and stays out of the batch. Being public does not give copy rights: borrow the shape only, never the words.
   - A **template** is a hook skeleton or post shape that reached the creator's top 10% by views at least twice, with two different fills. Posts whose text is the same after ignoring case, punctuation and spacing, or that differ only in a number such as an age or a year, are one fill, so a reposted or recycled post counts once. Write the skeleton with placeholders, for example `i [did X] for [time]. here's what [result]:`. A word is a **fixed word** only when every evidence post has it in the same place: the same word count from the start of the line, or from its end (compare words ignoring case and punctuation). That covers the shared opening, the shared ending and a shared word between placeholders, such as "is" in `the longer i build the more i see [thing] is [what it is].` when every [thing] is one word. A shared word that moves around inside the fills stays inside a placeholder: "your" in "…rest is the secret weapon of your best days." and "…silence is your answer." is part of `[what it is]`. Every word that is not fixed belongs to a placeholder. These positions only find the skeleton: a new fill may have any number of words, so `[thing]` above may be two words in a new post.
   - For each template show its evidence: per post its first line, date, views, media type, part count, fill and whether its text was complete, plus the top 10% line it beat. A shape that hit the top 10% only once, or only with one fill, is "unproven". Label it that way and never use it in this batch.
   - This workflow writes text posts. If a template's evidence posts are image, carousel or video posts (`media_type` other than `TEXT_POST`), the picture was part of what went viral. Label the template "needs media" and leave it out of this batch.
   - Check each template is not **tired**: call `search_posts` with the longest stretch of fixed words between placeholders (most words; the first on a tie), as written in the evidence posts, as the query (search matches apostrophes and quote marks exactly, so when the stretch has one, search it as written, then with every mark straight (`'` `"`) and with every mark curly (`’` `“` `”`), skipping a form you already searched, and merge the results; record every query you searched, in order, as the template's `tired_check_queries` in the receipt, or an empty list when there is no stretch to search), with `sort_by: "recent"` and a `limit` of at least its `total_matched`. If fewer posts come back than `total_matched`, raise the limit and call again. If a raised limit still brings back fewer, search again with `to` set to the oldest date that came back and merge the results by post id, until you hold every match. Keep only the returned posts whose text fits the skeleton, then take its three most recent uses by date. A reposted copy counts as a use here, because the audience saw it again; record that rule and the three uses (date, views, fill) in the receipt. If the skeleton has no stretch of at least two fixed words, the search cannot find its uses: label it "tired check incomplete". If all three fell under the top 10% line, the audience has had enough of it for now. Label it "tired", show those three view counts, and pick another template; this holds even when more uses may be hidden. Otherwise, if you cannot see every match, label it "tired check incomplete" and say so at the gate.
   - **Calibrate the fresh-reader check** before any `generate_content` call, and only when drafting will go ahead: a run without the drafting yes stops after the tired check, with no control and no waiver question. For each template you may use, give a fresh reader (rule 4) the template's most-viewed evidence post on its own, with the same prompt the new posts will get. Record who read it, its answer word for word and its verdict. If the creator's own winner is not a clear yes, the check is stricter than what went viral for them on this template. Show the creator that post and the answer, and ask: waive the fresh-reader check for this template in this run, or leave the template out. Until they waive it, label the template "control failed" and leave it out. With no fresh reader available, the control is unavailable too and nothing is asked: read the post yourself with the same prompt, record your answer word for word with its `DELIVERS:` word as the verdict and `unavailable` as the result, and show "control: unavailable (self-read: <your answer>)" at the gate.
   - A template with none of the labels above is "eligible". Step 3 labels the ones it picks "used". When more than one label applies, the template carries the first of them in this order: shape unverified, media unknown, needs media, unproven, tired, control failed, no fitting fact. Show the others next to it as "also: <labels>" and record them, in that order, as `also_labels`. "Tired check incomplete" is a flag, not a label.
3. **Write.** Pick up to three templates, each one different. Leave out every template labelled unproven, tired, needs media, media unknown, shape unverified or control failed. Label each template you pick "used", even when none of its drafts survives the checks. A "tired check incomplete" template may be used only with that flag shown next to it at the gate. For each, call `query_brain` for the real facts that fill its placeholders. Before any `generate_content` call, fill the hook yourself with each fact in its exact Brain wording and count the characters exactly as the hook will be posted: the fact keeps its words and their order, while its letter case and closing mark follow the template (in a lowercase template the fact is lowercase too, except a word the creator writes in capitals, such as AI, and the fact's own closing mark gives way to the skeleton's, never doubled); record each fact as one row of the template's `hook_fit` in the receipt, with its `fact`, `filled_hook`, `filled_hook_chars` (the count), `limit` and `after_tool_calls` (the number of `tool_path` entries made before you counted, never past the first `generate_content`). Use only facts whose filled hook is within the limit. **Repeat check:** for each fact that fits, its claim words are every word of the fact except the template's fixed words and these small words: a, an, the, is, are, was, were, be, of, to, in, on, for, at, by, with, from, and, or, but, not, no, so, too, very, i, you, we, my, your, our, it, its, this, that, all, every, he, she, they, them, me, i'm, it's, do, don't, does, has, have, can, will, just, more, most, than, what, about, how, when, if. Call `search_posts` once for each claim word, on its own (for example `comfort`, then `slow`, then `leak`, for "comfort is a slow leak."): a phrase misses a post that makes the same claim in other words. Get every match, as in the tired check, read each one for the claim, and stop once one makes it. A word with more than 200 matches is too broad to read in full: read only the matches where that word starts a claim (it is followed by is, isn't, are, aren't, was, will, won't, can, can't, do, don't, does, doesn't, has, have, should, makes or means), and read every match of at least one other claim word in full. If you cannot get every match of a word, or the fact's only claim word is that broad, its repeat check is incomplete: leave the fact out, with `skipped` set to `repeat check incomplete`. A fact whose claim one of the creator's own posts already makes, in any template or in none, is a repeat: leave it out, with `skipped` set to `repeat` and that post's date. Record on that fact's `hook_fit` row its `repeat_check`: `searches`, in order, each with its `word`, `total` (its `total_matched`), `returned` (the posts you got, after any paging), `read` (the ones you read for the claim) and, when `read` is under `returned`, `narrowed_by` (the rule you used); `matches`, the posts that make the claim (date, views, first line and `found_by`, the word that found it); and, for a fact that passes, `closest`, the post that came nearest (date, views, its line and `why_not`, why its claim differs), or `none`. You decide whether a post makes the claim; never leave that call to the creator. Also skip a fact that already fails a step-4 check before any call (for example it repeats a fill from the evidence or the tired check, its line would be longer than the evidence allows, or it is not what the placeholder names, such as a fact about sleep in a slot for a money lesson), record that check as the row's `skipped` and say it at the gate. When no fact fits, relabel the template "no fitting fact", leave it out and make no `generate_content` call for it. Then call `generate_content` with the skeleton written out line for line, the hook's character limit (80, or the proven hook's length when that is longer), each fact in its exact Brain wording, and the rule "keep this skeleton exactly; change only the placeholder words; keep each fact's words and the link between them". Use `contentType: "long-form"` for a thread template and `"short-form"` for a single post. Make up to two versions per template (one call each, for example with a different style preset), give each only facts that fit, and keep the draft id each call returns and the fact it was given. Record whether each version's text keeps that fact as `fact_kept`: `word for word` (letter case and the closing mark aside), `shortened` (step 4 allows it only when the claim stays the same) or `changed` (a new claim, which fails), with the version's first line as `hook` so the label can be checked against it. Before each call, write a key unique to this run, template and version (for example `<run id>-<template id>-a`) in the receipt and pass it as `idempotency_key`. If a call errors or times out, retry only with that same key, and count the call against the number the creator said yes to. Pick the best one per template and say why in one line. Never make more calls than the creator said yes to. If a version comes back off the skeleton, it counts as a failed version, not a draft to fix by hand.
4. **Check every final post.** A post must pass all of these before it can be shown for approval. Each check is pass or fail: "pass with a flag" or "the creator should confirm" is a fail. Two results are neither: a fresh-reader check the creator waived for this template in step 2 is recorded as "fail, waived", and one with no fresh reader available is recorded as "unavailable" (rule 4). A post with either may be proposed, with that result shown at the gate.
   - `validate_post` returns valid for the exact parts, in order.
   - The skeleton matches the template line for line (same line count and shape), and no line after the hook is longer, in characters, than the longest version of that line among the template's evidence posts (the hook has its own limit, next).
   - The hook is 80 characters or less, or no longer than the template's proven hook.
   - The fresh-reader check is a clear yes: the body gives what the hook promised, with nothing that needs outside context. On a template the creator waived in step 2, the reader still reads the post and its answer goes to the gate, marked "fail, waived" unless it is a clear yes. A waiver covers that template in this run only, and it is not a yes to schedule: the post still needs the yes at the gate.
   - The post is text only, and its template needs no media.
   - It reads at the energy of its evidence posts (their own swear words, short lines), not flat next to them. A fix that drops the creator's own swear words or intensifiers from a fact fails. Record a one-line reason for every energy result.
   - A thread has every part present and in order.
   - Every number, quote and story traces to the Brain, the creator's posts or this chat. A fact may be shortened to fit a slot only if its claim stays the same; changing who or what it is about, or the link between its parts ("is the secret to" becoming "is", "leads to" becoming "equals"), makes a new claim, and that fails. A shortening that drops a word the sentence needs, such as an article or a verb, also fails.
   - The filled-in words are new: they differ from every evidence post and every use found in the tired check. The same subject and idea in the same slot is a repeat and fails, and so is a fact whose claim one of the creator's own posts already makes, in any template or in none (the step-3 repeat check).
   - No two posts in the batch share a hook or a template.
   If a post fails, say which check failed. Fix it by hand at most once, and only when the fix keeps the skeleton and the facts (for example a shorter hook), then check again and say what changed. If you would ask the creator to confirm the fix, it fails. If the fixed post still fails, drop it. Writing it again needs a new drafting yes that names the extra `generate_content` calls and the drafts they save; without that yes, drop the post from the batch.
5. **Plan the times.** Call `best_time_to_post` for the best slots. Call `list_scheduled_posts` for the window. The number of posts it returns must equal its `total_in_window`. If it does not, raise the `limit` and call it again. Never treat a short list as a free calendar. Every proposed slot must be at least 2 hours inside the window you listed (2 hours after its start and 2 hours before its end), because a post queued just outside the window can still be within 2 hours of a slot; to plan further out, list a longer window first. Every slot must be at least 5 minutes in the future (Threadify refuses a sooner time). Give each post its own slot that is at least 2 hours clear of anything already queued and of the other new posts, and make sure no new post repeats text already queued. The list shows only a preview of each queued post, so for every queued post whose preview ends in "…" or that has more than one part, call `get_schedule_status` once for its full text in order. Compare every part of every new post with those full texts and the full previews. If a full text cannot be fetched, the comparison is unresolved: no post is proposed until it is fetched. If a queued post already uses the same template, say so at the gate and keep the new one on a different day.
6. **Approval gate.** Show, in chat, in plain text:
   - the account handle and timezone;
   - each template with its label, the top 10% line it beat, and its evidence posts: per post its first line, date, views, media type, part count, fill and whether its text was complete, and any plug part left out; for a tired template, the view counts of its last three uses;
   - per template its hook fit: each fact, its filled hook and its length against the limit, its repeat check (each claim word with its match count and any narrowing, then the post that already makes the claim or the closest one), and every fact left out before drafting, with the check it failed (too long, a repeat of one of the creator's posts, a repeat check incomplete, or another step-4 check);
   - the exact full text of every part of every post;
   - the check results for each post, each pass or fail, with the hook's length against its limit (for example 78/81 when the proven hook is 81 characters), the energy reason and the fresh reader's answer; a waived check reads "fresh reader: fail, waived by you for <template-id> (your words: "...")";
   - the media for each post: none, as this workflow schedules text posts only;
   - the global auto-repost setting exactly as read: "on: Threadify re-shares each post <delay> after it goes out" (or "after <n> likes"), "off", or "unknown". This workflow never changes it. If it is unknown, nothing can be scheduled until the creator checks it in Threadify;
   - for each post fixed by hand: "schedules the text shown here; saved draft <id> keeps the old wording";
   - every draft saved so far, by id, each marked proposed, excluded or not picked;
   - for each template, its control (the fresh reader's answer on its most-viewed evidence post) and any waiver, in the creator's words;
   - each post's fingerprint and stable idempotency key (step 7.3 says how they are built), so the yes binds them;
   - the exact date and time for each post, in the creator's timezone;
   - the action: schedule, never publish now.
   Ask for one yes on this exact batch. Nothing is scheduled without it. Any change to text or time needs a new yes for that post. If the host holds the call for approval, say where to approve it in one line and wait. Never route around it.

   If the creator sends a post back with a note, change only what the note names and keep the skeleton: a new call-out or a new fact stays on the same template. If the note says the post is flat or boring, or rejects the skeleton, switch to another eligible template; polishing the words of a boring post does not fix it. A new `generate_content` call needs a drafting yes that names the extra calls and drafts. Show the changed post at a new gate; the yes on the other posts stands.
7. **Schedule after the yes.** For each approved post, in order:
   1. **Recheck.** Call `get_connection_defaults` and confirm the same account, timezone and global auto-repost setting as the gate showed; a changed or unknown setting blocks every post until a new yes. Call `list_scheduled_posts` again for a window that starts at least 2 hours before this post's slot and ends at least 2 hours after it, with the list length equal to `total_in_window`, and recheck the 2-hour clearance, duplicate text (at every dispatch, fetch the full text again with `get_schedule_status` for every queued post whose preview ends in "…" or that has more than one part, because an edit can change a later part without changing the preview or part count; never reuse text fetched earlier) and same-template days. A post that now conflicts is **blocked**: say why, propose a new time and wait for a new yes for that post. Never move an approved time yourself.
   2. A slot that is now less than 5 minutes away is blocked: it needs a new time and a new yes. Call `validate_post` again on the exact parts.
   3. **Save the intent first.** The fingerprint is the sha256 of the compact JSON array `[account, parts, media, scheduled_at]` (as `JSON.stringify` writes it): the account (step 1), every part's exact text in order as plain strings (not `{text}` objects), `[]` for media, and the approved time as an ISO instant written `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ss±hh:mm` in the creator's timezone (no milliseconds, never `Z`; for example `2026-06-03T09:00:00-04:00`). Any other form of the same time makes a different fingerprint. The stable idempotency key is `viral-posts:<account>:<template-id>:<first 12 characters of the fingerprint>`. Recompute both; they must equal what the gate showed. Save them in the receipt as pending. A changed time or text makes a new fingerprint and needs a new yes.
   4. Call `schedule_post` with that key as `idempotency_key`, `scheduled_at` as that same approved time string, `account` as the account from step 1, the matching `content_type` (`short-form` for one post, `long-form` for a thread), and the exact approved text: `posts` as `[{"text": ...}]` objects, one per part in order, or `text` for a single post. Never pass `draft_id`: a saved draft can be edited after the gate, and the tool does not say which text wins when `draft_id` is passed. Keep the draft id in the receipt as where the text came from. Leave out `platforms` (so it goes to Threads only), `auto_plug` and the per-post `auto_repost`; the creator's global auto-repost setting still applies, as shown at the gate. Save the returned scheduled post id in the receipt.
   5. If the call errors or times out, the result is unknown. Look for the post with `list_scheduled_posts` before any retry, and retry only with the same key; never make a new key to force a second write. A clear failure on one post does not stop the others; an unknown one stays on hold until you find it or rule it out.
   6. **Read back.** Call `get_schedule_status` for the returned id. Report "scheduled", never "published", only when its status shows the post queued and its account, every part's text and its time match what was approved. Otherwise report "schedule unverified" and do not retry blindly.

Each post ends in one state: proposed, excluded (its template is unproven, tired, needs media, media unknown or shape unverified, or control failed, or the post failed a check), blocked (a queue conflict, repeated or unresolved queued text, or a changed auto-repost setting), approved, scheduled or schedule unverified.

This workflow never publishes now, never deletes and never edits other queued posts.

## Local fallback

Without a working connection, ask the creator to paste their best posts with view counts and dates (the more the better, so you can see the top 10% line), the full text of every part of each post you will copy a shape from, their 10 most recent posts with view counts, and the facts each new post may use. A post that ends in "…" or is missing thread parts is cut off: ask for the full text. A paste that gives only each post's first part leaves every post cut off until its full text, or the creator's word that it had one part, comes in. Until you have it, label that template "shape unverified" and leave it out of the batch. Ask how many posts they have published in all: the top 10% line is the view count of the pasted post ranked at a tenth of that total, rounded down and at least 1 (rank 370 of 3,700; rank 370 of 3,709; rank 1 for 1 to 19 posts). If they have published none, there is nothing to copy: say so and stop. If the paste stops before that rank, use its lowest view count and call the line estimated. Until they tell you the total, do the same, and say at the gate which labels would change if the line were higher. Ask whether each template post had a picture; a template whose posts did is "needs media", and one with no answer yet is "media unknown" at the gate. The tired check uses every dated use of the template in any paste: find them with the same stretch of fixed words and quote-mark forms as step 2, and record those stretches as `tired_check_queries`; recent posts with no text cannot show a use, so they count as missing. A paste of only best posts hides a tired template, so if the recent posts are missing, label the template "tired check incomplete". Find the templates, run the step-2 control before you fill any (with no fresh agent it is unavailable and no waiver is asked; with one, the control and waiver work as in step 2), count the hook fit as in step 3 (record `hook_fit`; a template with no fitting fact is labelled "no fitting fact" and gets no post), run the step-3 repeat check on every pasted post instead of `search_posts`, looking for the same claim words one at a time (record `repeat_check` with `in` set to `pasted posts` and, per word, the pasted posts that have it as its `total`, `returned` and `read`), then ask the creator to search their own posts for each claim word of each fact you will use and paste every match, or say there is none (record their answer as `creator_search` in that fact's `repeat_check`, or `no answer`; with no answer, show "repeat check: pasted posts only" on that post at the gate and record it as that post's `repeat_gate_line`), fill them yourself from those facts only, run the same checks (skip `validate_post` and say so; check the 500 characters per part rule by hand) and propose times from the posting times the creator gives, marked "tentative, not checked against your live calendar" (with no times given, propose none and ask for them). Label every draft "written locally, not by Threadify". Never claim Threadify generated, validated or scheduled anything.

## Receipt

Save a receipt in the creator's private workspace that follows `workflow-receipt.v1` (`workflow_id`, `adapter`, `account_handle`, `action`, `approved_text`, `approved_text_sha256`, `status`, `timestamp`, `timezone`, `tool_path`, `fallback_used`). `approved_text` is a list of every part of every approved post, in order: `[]` until the creator's yes at the gate, with `approved_text_sha256` set to `none-awaiting-approval`; after the yes, `approved_text_sha256` is the SHA-256 of that exact list. Without a connection, `account_handle` is the handle the creator gave you, or `unconnected`. `tool_path` lists every tool called, in order, including a `get_connection_defaults` call that failed and sent you to the fallback; it is empty only when no tool was called. Every template has a `template_id`. Add the global auto-repost setting as the gate showed it and as each dispatch read it; the templates with their label and any `also_labels`, media decision and tired-check queries, and per evidence post its first line, date, views, media type, part count, fill and whether its text was complete; the drafting yes and the draft ids saved, each with the fact it was given and its `fact_kept`; per template its `hook_fit` (each fact, its filled hook, the character count, the limit and `after_tool_calls`, and for each fact that fits its `repeat_check` and any `skipped` check); the check results per post, with the hook length and its limit, the energy reason and the fresh reader (who read it, its answer word for word and its verdict); per template its fresh-reader control (who read it, its answer word for word, its verdict and `after_tool_calls`, the number of `tool_path` entries made before it ran, which is never past the first `generate_content`) and any `fresh_reader_waiver` (`template_id`, `creator_words`, `at`; this run only); the schedule proposal with the calendar window you listed and its live total, and each dispatch's window and total; and per post the state, fingerprint, idempotency key, returned scheduled post id and readback result. `status` is exactly one of: `awaiting_approval` (posts proposed, no yes yet; `approved_text` is empty), `nothing_to_approve` (every post excluded or blocked), `drafting_declined` (no drafting yes; `action` is `evidence_only`), `fallback_ready` (the local fallback, whether or not any post survives; `action` is `local_packet` and `fallback_used` is true), `scheduled` (every approved post read back as scheduled), `partially_scheduled` (some approved posts scheduled, others blocked, failed or unverified) or `schedule_unverified` (no approved post read back as scheduled). In every other case `action` is `schedule_post`. Keep the creator's posts, numbers and receipts out of any public place.
