# 5. Receipts

## Principle

A receipt is a true, specific detail that shows you were there: a number, a moment, an object, something someone said. Trust on Threads comes from receipts, not from sounding confident.

Two rules:

1. **Be specific.** "Many", "some", "recently" and "significant" tell the reader nothing.
2. **Never fake it.** A made-up detail is worse than a vague one. If you cannot back a claim, soften it ("in my experience", "the pattern i keep noticing") or cut it.

Check these before they go out: follower counts, money, client results, hours spent, named people, and anything about health or the law.

## Worked example

An example written for this lesson, not a real post or a real result.

Vague:

> i've helped many creators grow recently.

With a receipt:

> last week a client sent me her first post in two years.
> 41 words. it took her three days to press publish.

The second version only works if it is true. In the learner's post, every detail has to be theirs.

## Common mistake

Decorating with numbers. A big round number in the first line that is there to impress, not because it happened.

## On the learner's posts

Connected: use `greatest_hits` and ask the learner to underline every specific detail in their best posts. Then do the same on a weak one from `read_post_performance`. Compare the count.

Not connected: same exercise on the pasted sample.

You cannot verify the learner's claims. Ask "is that exactly true?" and take their answer.

## Practice task

Write down five true, specific things from your own work in the last month. Small is fine. These are your receipts for the next few posts.

## Lesson post

Write a post built on one receipt from your list. Every number and detail must be true.
