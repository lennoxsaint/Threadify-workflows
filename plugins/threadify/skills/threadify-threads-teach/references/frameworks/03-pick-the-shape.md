# 3. Pick the shape

## Principle

Choose the shape of a post for what you want it to do, not out of habit.

| You want | Shape that fits | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New people to see you | A short post | One clean thought is easy to read and easy to pass on |
| A point that needs one more beat | A two-part post | The first post stays clean, the second explains |
| People to save and use it | A short list | Clear steps, fewer items than your first draft |
| People to trust you | A story | A real moment with something at stake |

Default to short. Go longer only when the idea needs a story, proof or steps. If the idea is thin, shorten the post. Do not stretch it.

Observation usually travels further than instruction. A how-to can live in the body, under a stronger first line.

## Worked example

An example written for this lesson, not a real post. One idea, written as a short post because the goal is to be seen by new people:

> the post you were scared to publish is usually the one people reply to.

The same idea as a story would start with the night you nearly deleted it. As a list it would be three signs a post is worth the nerves. Same idea, different job.

## Common mistake

Writing a long teaching thread by default. Length gets used to make a thin idea look like effort.

## On the learner's posts

Connected: use `read_post_performance` to look at their recent posts. Sort them by shape with the learner and ask which shape they use most, and whether it matches what their mission needs. Report only numbers the tool returned.

Not connected: sort the pasted sample by shape. You cannot say which shape performs best for them, so say that.

## Practice task

Pick one idea. Write one line for each of the four shapes saying what that version would be. Then choose the one that fits your mission this week and say why.

## Lesson post

Write the idea in the shape you chose.

## Edge of the free material

This lesson gives the four shapes and how to choose. It does not include performance breakdowns by format. Use the one-line pointer if the learner asks for them.
