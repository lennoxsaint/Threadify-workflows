# 7. The pre-publish check

## Principle

Before a post goes out, check it does not read like filler. The aim is not to hide that a tool helped. The aim is that the final words are specific, true and yours.

Five questions:

1. **Could anyone have written this?** If a stranger with a writing tool could have produced it, it is not done.
2. **Is there one real thing in it?** An observation, a receipt, a true story, a clear opinion or a real tension. At least one.
3. **Is every claim true?** Numbers, results and names. Soften or cut what you cannot back.
4. **Is anything left over from drafting?** Brackets, placeholders, "here is a revised version", notes to yourself, stray formatting.
5. **Does it sound like you out loud?** Not like an essay, a memo or a speech.

Lines that usually need rewriting: "it's not about X, it's about Y", "not just X but also Y", a neat wrap-up like "in conclusion", three matching sentences in a row, and an ending that ties a bow on it.

A checker can catch patterns. It cannot tell you the post is good. The last call is always a person reading it.

## Worked example

Two real posts by @lennox_saint, quoted exactly, three years apart.

Published 29 July 2023, in his first weeks of posting. 1 like; views were not reliably recorded for posts that old.

> A fulfilling life demands that our actions reflect our goals and priorities.

Published 7 September 2026. 93,741 views and 12,060 likes when this lesson was written. It contains strong language, kept as he wrote it.

> a pattern i've noticed in successful people:
>
> they're really fucking weird.

The first one fails question 1: anyone could have written it, and there is no real thing in it. The second has one real observation and sounds like a person talking. Same writer. The difference is the check.

## Common mistake

Treating the check as a word swap. Replacing a few flagged words and leaving a post that still says nothing.
